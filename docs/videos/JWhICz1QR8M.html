Why Graph Engineering will 10x your Claude/Codex

Channel: Greg Isenberg · Published: 2026-08-03 · Length: 26 min · Watch on YouTube

TL;DR

Graph engineering is the design of work around AI, not a new prompting trick. Prompt engineering improves the request and context engineering improves the information. Graph engineering maps the jobs, state, handoffs, parallel paths, checks, loops, and human gates that make a multi-step workflow reliable.

Walkthrough

The core vocabulary is intentionally simple. Jobs are units of work, arrows describe dependencies, and state is the shared record passed between steps. This distinguishes an agent graph from a knowledge graph. A knowledge graph helps a model understand relationships in information. An agent graph governs how a process moves among planning, research, checking, synthesis, and human judgment.

A graph is warranted when work has multiple steps or sources, risks, parallel paths, approvals, or a need to revisit prior work. The example is an AI bookkeeping product for Shopify merchants: rather than one oversized prompt, map the research, analysis, assumptions, risk checks, decision criteria, and approval points. The same principle applies to support, content, and coding.

Start manually. Draw the flow in a simple tool, give each step a lane, and run it with separate agents or files. Only when the shape proves useful should it become a LangGraph workflow with state checkpoints, an n8n or Make automation that connects business systems, or another managed implementation. The objective is the smallest graph that improves quality, not a visually impressive workflow.

A crucial quality control is separating writer and checker. A model grading its own work tends to inflate confidence. In support, a checker validates accuracy, tone, policy, and risk. In content, it checks specificity and pacing. In coding, separate planning, implementation, review, tests, UI checks, edge cases, and human approval.

Why this matters for my work

This is directly applicable to agentic BI, marketing processes, and the vault's skill design. I could take one recurring workflow, draw its state and gates, run it manually, and only then decide whether an automation platform is warranted.
