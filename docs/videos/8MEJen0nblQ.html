Why Your AI Offer Isn't Selling, and How to Fix That

Channel: Nate Herk | AI Automation · Published: 2026-07-06 · Length: 56 min · Watch on YouTube

TL;DR: Nate B. Jones argues the reason so many AI offers don't sell isn't the technology, it's that builders are selling AI itself instead of the transformation it produces. He gives a working definition of 'AI-native,' a real anecdote about how storytelling wins deals that pure technical credentials can't, and concrete advice for finding and connecting with a specific underserved niche.

What 'AI-native' actually means. Asked to define the term plainly, Jones says the pattern he sees across both individual contributors and business leaders is that AI-native people 'reach for artificial intelligence first, second, and last,' not because they reject deterministic code or dismiss human judgment, but because they treat AI as a supertool to reach for before anything else, 'almost like wearing an AI mech suit all the time.' It's a mindset, not a specific tool stack, and it applies as much to a solo operator as to an executive running a large org.

The core diagnosis: you're selling AI, not the outcome. Jones states it directly: 'AI is not valuable in and of itself. Sell that story. Sell the transformation. Sell the real impact of what you're delivering.' His concrete example uses an HVAC company: every one he's called struggles with answering phones fast enough and getting dispatch right, usually because one overworked person in the back office is drowning in calls. The pitch isn't 'we implement AI,' it's 'I make sure your phone always gets answered and every customer's concern gets heard,' a specific, felt pain point that any business owner immediately understands, with AI simply being the mechanism underneath.

The storytelling-wins-deals anecdote. In a small legal firm's AI vendor search, every competing provider showed up with identical language, security certifications, compliance guarantees, 'trust us.' The vendor that actually won the deal walked in, physically set a server on the table, and said, 'your data lives here and it won't leave.' Jones's point isn't that on-premises hosting is always the right answer, it's that a concrete, physically graspable story beat a stack of abstract compliance claims that all sounded identical. His broader claim: 'if you are having trouble selling, the issue is that you are having trouble storytelling,' and he treats cultivating storytelling as a skill worth investing in as deliberately as cultivating technical build skill.

Why human connection becomes the scarce asset, not the AI itself. Looking five years out toward a world of overwhelmingly agent-to-agent traffic online, Jones argues the ability to build a genuine human connection and tell a story people actually want to hear directly from you becomes 'currency that you can't debase... it's gold,' precisely because it can't be inflated away by adding more agents to the internet. His practical advice for anyone trying to break into business consulting with no track record yet: get specific about a niche (his example: HVAC companies in one city, Seattle), because that demographic isn't competing for attention against a swarm of AI-generated content, and the right non-obvious tactics, from targeted local ads to literally offering free work to get in the door, only become clear once you deeply understand that one specific customer.

On tackling an unfamiliar domain. Asked how to approach a problem entirely outside your own expertise (his own example: movie production, which he knows nothing about), Jones's method is to first study what AI-native practitioners in that space are already doing, then separately study what deep domain experts in that space are doing with AI, and combine both perspectives. If a solved tool already exists, call it; if the problem is genuinely unsolved, that's actually the better outcome, because building the solution input by input turns it into real intellectual property you can use yourself or eventually sell to others.

Why this matters for my work: the core reframe, sell the transformation and the specific outcome, never the AI mechanism itself, is directly usable language for Ranger AI's positioning and for the 'Beyond Chat' course's marketing copy. The niche-first advice (a specific underserved demographic beats broad AI-agency positioning) and the 'AI-native = reach for AI first, second, last' definition are both clean, quotable framings worth testing directly in Amir's own consulting pitch and course marketing.
