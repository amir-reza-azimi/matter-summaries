You don't have to review every AI-generated PR

Channel: How I AI · Published: 2026-08-08 · Length: 1 min · Watch on YouTube

TL;DR

The goal is not to eliminate review of AI-generated pull requests. It is to make review proportional to risk. An agent reads a change, scores it against explicit criteria, and routes medium- and high-risk work to a human approver.

What the review agent checks

The video frames the agent as a lightweight combination of instructions and skills, often written in markdown. After reading a change, it scores the change surface and blast radius, reversibility, whether it touches data security, whether security coverage exists, operational impact, and the verification gap.

The verification gap is especially useful. It asks whether tests and CI actually establish that the change is safe, not simply whether they happened to run. A change can look small and still be risky if its effects are hard to test, if it reaches sensitive data, or if rolling it back would be expensive.

Make the score auditable

The scoring rule should be a routing aid, not an assertion that the model knows risk objectively. Each dimension needs a plain-language definition and examples of a low, medium, and high score. The output should state which evidence raised the score and which uncertainty remains. That gives an engineer something to challenge, instead of asking them to trust a single opaque number.

Start conservatively. A low score should only skip full review when a change is narrow, reversible, covered by relevant checks, and outside sensitive systems. The policy also needs hard stops that override the total, such as authentication, access controls, payments, personal data, migrations, production infrastructure, or a missing test path. Those categories are about accountability, not only likelihood of a defect.

Risk thresholds create an operating rule

The example uses clear thresholds: below 24 points is low risk, 25 through 64 is medium risk, and 65 or more is high risk. Medium and high risk require human approval. The exact numbers matter less than the existence of an explicit policy. A team can inspect, challenge, and refine a score-based rule far more easily than an undefined instruction to be careful.

This prevents the usual extremes. If every change needs full review, the agent creates a backlog. If nothing needs review, responsibility is unclear. A risk tier gives low-risk work a fast lane while preserving accountable oversight for work with meaningful consequences. Track the reviewer overrides as well. They reveal whether thresholds are too loose, too conservative, or simply blind to a recurring context cue.

Why this matters for my work

The same pattern works beyond pull requests. For an analytics agent, dimensions could include data sensitivity, customer impact, reversibility, evidence quality, and whether a recommendation triggers a real action. I could define a conservative initial rubric for one workflow and use it as an advisory layer before allowing automatic execution.
