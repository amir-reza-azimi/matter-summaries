The $1,000/hour Solo AI business (Full Course)

Channel: Greg Isenberg · Published: 2026-07-20 · Length: 61 min · Watch on YouTube

TL;DR

Corey Ganon gives away his full playbook for a solo AI-consulting business: a $999 "AI tools assessment" that opens the door to a recurring AI-concierge retainer worth over $1,000 an hour, plus seven ways to find clients without an existing audience or budget. It's a near-complete productized-service blueprint, built almost entirely around Claude.

Walkthrough

The core offer is simple by design: a 45-minute structured interview with a small business owner (2 to 20 employees, $500K to $5M in revenue is the sweet spot) to surface bottlenecks, followed by a prescription of three to seven off-the-shelf AI tools that can reclaim five to ten hours a week. The guarantee is blunt: if he can't find at least five hours of weekly opportunity, the client gets their $999 back. Fulfillment runs in four phases. Phase one is the discovery call itself, recorded with an AI notetaker, where the only goal is extracting pain points, not pitching solutions. Phase two feeds that transcript to a Claude skill that researches off-the-shelf SaaS or AI tools that fix each pain point, with a manual quality-assurance pass to swap out anything mismatched for the client's size (his example: don't prescribe Salesforce to a four-person landscaping business). Phase three turns the findings into a client-facing report built in Claude Design, from a template he's giving away, structured around an effort-versus-impact matrix, a four-day quick-start plan, and a financial-impact slide quantifying exact monthly ROI. Phase four is a 30-minute review call walking through the report and asking three closing questions, including whether the client wants to implement themselves or hire him to do it — which converts roughly half of assessments into further paid work.

The upsell menu beyond the assessment includes process redesign (fixing a broken workflow before automating it, sold separately from the automation itself), simple Zapier/Make builds for cut-and-dried one-to-three-step processes, custom "knowledge systems" (his standout example: a custom GPT trained on a business listing's marketing package, cutting a business broker's 400-plus repetitive buyer emails to zero), and bespoke Claude skills for a client's proprietary process. His favorite, though, is the recurring "AI concierge" offer: two 45-minute monthly calls where he sits with a client and builds Claude Code skills live, starting clients at $1,200 a month and raising the price for each new signup as demand proved out, reaching $2,000 a month with five clients capped at six. The first call is always a guided onboarding — connecting tools, setting up context files and global instructions, showing what a scheduled task and a skill actually look like — so every call after that is more productive. Every workflow after that first call runs the same loop: audit how they do it today, optimize the number of steps, then automate it into a skill.

The client-acquisition section is the most immediately actionable part for anyone starting from zero: hosting a local "AI for business" meetup at a co-working space in exchange for free conference-room time; door-knocking, which he calls the single best way to learn sales; LinkedIn DMs that probe for pain rather than pitch in the first message; free mini-audits offered to people already in your personal network; partnering with agency-adjacent professionals (accountants, insurance agents, marketing consultants) who already serve the same client base and can refer AI questions your way; hosting free weekly "AI office hours" inside a co-working space's tenant community; and simply documenting small wins publicly as you go.

Standout moments

The reframe he keeps returning to is that "a confused mind doesn't buy... and a confused mind doesn't upsell" — the entire report is engineered to be embarrassingly simple to read, iterated on a dozen times just to cut and simplify further. And the pricing psychology tip is sharp: credit the $999 assessment fee toward a marked-up implementation package so the client feels like they're getting a discount while he keeps the same total revenue.

The retainer pricing mechanics are worth noting too: he intentionally raises the price for each new AI-concierge client as demand proves itself (going from $1,200 to $1,500 to $1,800 to $2,000 across his first five signups) and caps capacity deliberately at six clients, treating real scarcity as part of the sales conversation rather than a manufactured tactic. He also keeps a running Notion hub per client logging everything accomplished each session, which does double duty as retention: it's the visible proof of value that justifies the client continuing to pay every month, and it sets up the next upsell once the client's own stated 90-day success goal has already been hit early.

Why this matters for my work

This maps almost directly onto the amir-azimi consultancy and the 10x Operator course thesis: a full front-to-back template for productizing AI consulting, complete with a downloadable report template, pricing ladder, and a client-acquisition playbook that assumes zero existing audience. The AI-concierge structure in particular, teaching a business owner Claude Code and skills over two monthly calls, is close to a ready-made service offer I could pilot with a single client, using his audit-optimize-automate loop and his report template as the starting scaffold rather than building one from scratch.
