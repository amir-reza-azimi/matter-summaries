This AI Technology Will Replace Millions (Here's How to Prepare)

Channel: Nate Herk | AI Automation · Published: 2026-07-13 · Length: 14 min · Watch on YouTube

TL;DR: Past the clickbait framing about agentic AI replacing jobs, Nate delivers three concrete, non-technical Claude Code demos, each solving a job someone gets paid to do today, then distills a simple three-step framework for treating AI like a new hire you're onboarding rather than a tool you're operating.

The framing. Nate opens with the now-familiar 'you won't lose your job to AI, you'll lose it to someone using AI' line, and argues the shift is really about agentic AI: instead of doing every step yourself, you describe the outcome you want and the agent works out the steps. His core claim is that none of what follows requires learning to code, just learning to manage.

Demo one: a quarterly analytics dashboard from a single prompt. Using Claude Code's slash-goal feature (set a condition, let it keep working until met), he asked it to pull every video from Q2 2026, analyze comments, click-through rate and stats, and turn the insights into an Excel workbook. Claude reasoned and executed in a loop, pulled the data via a small Python script with the right API token, built a multi-tab workbook (executive dashboard, video scorecard, monthly trends, content pillars), and even screenshotted its own output to verify formatting and spacing before handing it back. The dashboard flagged real issues on its own, that impressions and CTR data was missing, and that topic concentration was a risk. The whole thing took about ten minutes, work Nate estimates would otherwise eat half a day.

Demo two: a working, persistent app built from a plain-English request. Pretending to run a small cleaning business, he asked Claude to build a job tracker with pay status and memory. It produced a working HTML app: jobs listed with dates, amounts owed, mark-as-paid and mark-as-done buttons, and the ability to delete a cancelled job, all of it persisting across page refreshes because he explicitly asked for memory. Nate's point isn't that the app is impressive engineering, it's that neither he nor most viewers could hand-write that HTML, but anyone can describe what they want in plain language, then iterate: change the colors, add a feature, turn it into a real hosted site or even a mobile app, just by asking.

Demo three: enriched lead generation and outreach copy. He set a goal for Claude to find 50 leads matching his exact customer profile inside Clay, enrich each one with business details, pain points, and Google review data, then draft a personalized outreach message for every single lead based on what it found. The output was a full spreadsheet: business, decision-maker, verified email, phone, location, review count, a specific 'pain point' column, a personalization hook, and a ready-to-send email subject and body for each of the 50 rows. One sample message referenced a specific one-star review about missed callbacks and offered a free 30-day trial tied directly to that complaint, genuinely usable cold-outreach copy, not generic filler. The full batch took about 20 minutes.

Standout moment, the reframe: 'Using Claude to me feels like I have a co-founder rather than just a virtual assistant... you now are able to manage a bunch of different super high-level employees, which makes you look ten times better.' The mindset shift he's pushing is explicit: you are not the operator anymore, you are the manager, and the skill to build is managing well, not executing.

The three-step onboarding framework. Step one: just start talking to it like you're onboarding a very smart new hire, explain what a good result looks like, what a bad one looks like, and let it get to know your context, the way you'd slowly ramp a new employee rather than dumping everything on them in week one. Step two: pick one real, recurring task you actually do, hand it to Claude, and don't accept the first output, correct it and keep iterating until it's something you'd genuinely use, because the ROI has to be felt on real work, not watched in someone else's demo. Step three: once that feels easy, start stacking bigger tasks and connecting Claude to the tools you actually use daily (Gmail, Slack, calendar), always keeping permissions explicit and asking-first, and set a measurable goal (three hours saved a week, 100 new leads a month) so you can track whether the system is actually working and improve it if it isn't.

Why this matters for my work: the three demos are close to a ready-made 'first win' menu for non-technical operators in the course, an analytics rollup, a tiny persistent tracker app, and enriched outreach, each achievable in under 20 minutes with plain-language prompting and each mapping to a real job function. The three-step onboarding framework (treat it like a new hire, prove ROI on one real task before scaling, then stack and connect tools with explicit permissions) is a clean, teachable structure worth lifting directly into the 'Beyond Chat' course material.
