TL;DR

This week's signal comes almost entirely from two people on Anthropic's own Claude Code team, Boris Cherny and Thariq Shihipar, clustering around two threads: concrete new tooling (a context-hygiene command, shareable artifact dashboards, a compact prompting heuristic) and a recurring observation about the gap between individuals who've 10x'd their output with Claude and organizations that haven't caught up yet. Ammaar Reshi, Logan Kilpatrick, and Jason Liu were all active this week too, but mostly in demo, product-news, or personal territory, with nothing from any of the three clearing the bar on its own.

Claude Code & tooling

Thariq Shihipar had the most concentrated run of practical posts this week. On prototyping discipline: build mockups, schemas, data models, and proof-of-concepts first, because that's the cheapest way to discover you don't actually want the output before burning a large token budget on the real thing — a habit worth encoding explicitly into any Claude Code workflow that starts from an ambiguous brief rather than a fully specified one. On prompting technique, he distilled his own practice into a compact heuristic: thin prompts, thick artifacts plus context, thin skills — meaning the prompt itself should stay short, the surrounding reference material and context should carry the real weight, and skills should stay narrowly scoped rather than becoming another place logic quietly sprawls into. And on Claude's artifact system specifically, he pointed out that artifacts have become expressive enough to combine in creative ways, one of his favorites being a live dashboard for a project inside Claude Tag that can be edited collaboratively by other people or driven directly from a local Claude Code session — a meaningfully different use case than a one-off chart or demo output.

Claude Code as a general agent

Two more from Thariq worth a direct look rather than just a mention. He live-tweeted editing his AI Engineer talk footage with Claude Code, feeding it four videos totaling roughly 60GB plus his HTML deck and a specific starting prompt, a solid concrete example of Claude Code being used as a general-purpose agent on a non-coding, non-trivial-file-size task. And earlier in the week he flagged that his AI Engineer World's Fair keynote, "A Field Guide to Fable," is now live on YouTube — the same field guide referenced secondhand in one of this week's video-digest passes, where "12 Hidden Settings to Enable in Claude Code" credits it directly as the source for its closing point on outcome-first prompting. Watching the primary source directly is worth doing rather than only getting it filtered through other creators' summaries of it.
