Trump announces $90 Medicare premium payments for eligible seniors

The White House said eligible Medicare Part B enrollees will receive a one-time $90 payment in October, with more than 20 million seniors potentially covered. President Trump announced the measure less than a month before the midterm elections, while eligibility excludes people already receiving certain premium assistance or enrolled in Medicare Advantage.

The payment is small beside typical premiums but politically meaningful because affordability has become a central campaign issue. The immediate questions are how quickly the payments arrive, how many beneficiaries qualify, and whether using the Medicare Improvement Fund establishes a precedent for election-year benefit interventions.

Brazil heads into a polarised presidential election

Brazilian voters are due to choose between incumbent President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva and Senator Flávio Bolsonaro, with only those two candidates considered likely to win. If no candidate clears 50%, the election will move to an October 25 runoff.

Latin America’s largest economy faces a contest with implications for crime policy, democratic institutions and relations with the United States. The immediate test is turnout and the margin in key states such as São Paulo, where the candidates made their final push before voting began.

Nearly 1,400 workers lose jobs at an Ohio truck factory

Nearly 1,400 workers were laid off at the former Navistar truck factory in Springfield, Ohio, days before Trump campaigned nearby. The scale of the cuts was large enough to erase roughly a year of job growth in the wider Dayton area, according to the Associated Press.

The factory is expected to be acquired by an armored-vehicle maker that says it aims to rebuild and eventually expand the workforce, but that offers little immediate certainty to displaced employees. The episode is a grounded indicator of how inflation, tariffs and industrial-policy promises are being experienced in a contested manufacturing region.

A $28 billion hemp-THC market faces a federal ban deadline

A federal-law loophole helped build a large market for intoxicating hemp-derived products, but Congress has moved to close it. The effective date was delayed from November 12 to December 11, giving companies and lawmakers a short window to seek a regulated alternative.

The dispute reaches beyond cannabis policy: it affects retailers, state tax bases, small manufacturers and a market that grew outside the regulated marijuana system. Industry advocates want age restrictions and potency limits, while opponents argue the products have been too lightly controlled. December is now the decision point.
