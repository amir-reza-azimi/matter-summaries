Nvidia replaces Jensen Huang with interim CEO Colette Kress

Nvidia announced Jensen Huang’s immediate resignation after reports of extramarital relationships, including one involving a woman suspected by American intelligence of spying for China. Finance chief Colette Kress becomes interim chief executive while the board considers internal and external candidates, and Harvey Jones becomes chairman. The departure ends Huang’s leadership of the company he co-founded in 1993. Beyond succession, the crisis puts chip exports and Nvidia’s relationships in Washington under scrutiny. The next test is whether the new leadership can reassure customers and employees while lawmakers examine the company’s access to China and the allegations surrounding its former chief.

Microsoft outage disrupts Outlook, Teams and government operations

An outage spread across Microsoft’s services, with nearly 130,000 Outlook problem reports on Downdetector at the peak and additional difficulties involving Teams, Copilot and Microsoft 365. Disruption continued into the night and affected businesses and government operations, including users in Asia. Microsoft said infrastructure was experiencing elevated load and that some servers failed automated health checks, while attempts to restore them were not immediately taking effect. Teams had been restored in North America, although users still reported problems. The incident illustrates how a shared workplace platform can transmit one infrastructure failure across many organisations’ communications and daily operations.

Canada gives Stellantis 30 days to answer funding breach allegations

Canada threatened to sue Stellantis and recover public funding after the automaker shifted planned production from Brampton, Ontario, to Illinois and agreed to sell the Canadian plant. Industry Minister Mélanie Joly said a formal default notice gives the company 30 days to address alleged breaches. More than 2,000 workers have been left without jobs after plans to build the Jeep Compass were abandoned. Ottawa had committed up to 529 million Canadian dollars to retool Brampton and Windsor. The dispute tests how governments can enforce manufacturing commitments attached to subsidies when tariffs encourage companies to move production across borders.

US employment holds up while home sales fall to a 16-year low

New American unemployment claims rose by 9,000 to 200,000, but remained consistent with a stable labour market, while continuing claims fell by 20,000 to one million, six hundred and ninety-seven thousand. Housing painted a much weaker picture: existing home sales dropped five and a half percent in October to an annual rate of three and three-quarter million, the lowest since August 2010. The median price nevertheless rose four tenths of a percent from a year earlier to $421,900. Together, the releases show how high borrowing costs can depress transactions without quickly reducing prices. Employment resilience also gives the Federal Reserve more room to concentrate on inflation rather than an immediate jobs downturn.

germany · notable

Germany leads EU oil reserves as new data show a wider emergency-stock decline

Eurostat’s newly published July figures show EU emergency oil stocks falling to one hundred and three and a half million tonnes, down two and a half percent from June and four point two percent from a year earlier. Commercial inventories moved in the opposite direction, rising to fifty-two and a half million tonnes. Germany held the largest amounts in both categories, with 20 million tonnes of emergency reserves and eleven point two million tonnes of commercial stocks. Thirteen member countries reported inventories below minimum emergency levels. These are historical data rather than a live shortage warning, but they matter because legally required reserves provide a buffer against import disruptions, alongside stocks held by commercial operators.

EU consults on how sustainability reports should be independently checked

The European Commission opened consultation on draft standards for limited assurance of corporate sustainability reporting, with responses due by January 18, 2027. Companies covered by the reporting rules must have their disclosures independently reviewed, but the simplification package removed the possibility of requiring the more demanding reasonable-assurance standard later. The consultation examines European audit oversight advice and adjustments to international standards. For companies, auditors and investors, the practical issue is what evidence an external reviewer must test before accepting a report. The outcome will shape both compliance costs and the credibility of information used to assess sustainability claims.

Indonesia’s vice president resigns after eligibility controversy

Gibran Rakabuming Raka resigned as Indonesia’s vice president after court rulings disputed whether his overseas education documents met election requirements and lawmakers began an impeachment process. President Prabowo Subianto accepted the resignation, while Raka denied that it was linked to his diploma and said his documents were complete. Raka is the son of former president Joko Widodo, making the controversy significant for relations between powerful political families. A vacancy does not trigger a national election: parliament must choose between two presidential nominees within 60 days. That process now determines who joins Prabowo’s government and whether the dispute produces further instability.

A Russian-owned Irish refinery continues shipping alumina to Russia because the material, used to make aluminium, is outside European sanctions. Brooklyn collapse prompts inspections at roughly 100 construction sites

New York ended rescue operations after a Brooklyn apartment building collapse killed eight people, including two children, and injured 18. Investigators had not established the cause, but the building fell while excavation was under way at an adjacent lot. Baltic fishing deal raises some catches while protecting depleted stocks

EU governments agreed 2027 Baltic fishing limits that increase permitted catches of several species, including sprat and central Baltic herring, while cod remains restricted to accidental by-catches. Western Baltic herring also largely stays under a by-catch-only limit, with an exception for small coastal fishers. The Commission warned that continuing targeted herring fishing and some recreational salmon fishing could undermine recovery and may conflict with the legal framework. The rules are due to apply from January. The agreement therefore offers additional access for some fleets while leaving a dispute over how far short-term fishing opportunities should yield to rebuilding depleted stocks.

world · notable

Armenia signs agreements to join EU customs and tax cooperation programmes

Armenia and the European Union signed agreements enabling Armenian authorities to participate in the Customs and Fiscalis programmes alongside member states and other participating countries. The arrangements provide access to joint activities, training and administrative cooperation rather than EU membership or a new trade agreement. Fiscalis supports work against tax fraud, evasion and aggressive tax planning, while the Customs programme addresses trade flows, technology and security threats. The agreements give Armenia’s stated ambition for closer European ties an operational component. Their practical value will depend on authorities using shared expertise to improve enforcement and reduce administrative burdens for legitimate trade.
