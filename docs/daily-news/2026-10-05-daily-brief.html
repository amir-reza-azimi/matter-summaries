world · notable

Brazil’s presidential contest moves to an October 25 runoff

Flávio Bolsonaro and incumbent President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva will face each other in a runoff after neither secured the outright majority required in Brazil’s first round. With nearly all votes counted, Bolsonaro led the initial tally, but both candidates now have three weeks to court voters who backed neither finalist.

The result puts Latin America’s largest economy at a consequential political fork. A Bolsonaro victory would strengthen Washington’s influence, while Lula has prioritised distance from the US and deeper ties with China. Affordability, corruption allegations and the direction of the Amazon will all be central to the final campaign.

Bosnian Serb nationalist Dodik claims victory for his party

Milorad Dodik said his nationalist party had won decisively in Bosnia’s election, although he was not personally on the ballot. Early results showed his ally leading for a presidency seat in the Serb-majority entity, while turnout was lower than in 2022.

The outcome matters because Dodik’s separatist rhetoric has repeatedly tested the post-war settlement and slowed Bosnia’s path toward EU membership. His continued influence, despite a ban from public office, keeps ethnic tensions and regional security concerns high while Bosnia tries to make its fractured institutions work.

Germany announces €1.3 billion Ukraine package during Merz visit to Kyiv

Chancellor Friedrich Merz announced more than €1.3 billion in new assistance for Ukraine, including roughly €1 billion in military aid and €350 million for energy resilience. The package includes long-range strike weapons, drones, satellite technology, generators and repair support ahead of winter.

The visit makes Germany’s role more tangible at a moment when attacks on Ukrainian cities are intensifying. It also links military support to industrial and energy cooperation: Ukraine and Germany signed agreements covering security, weapons production and counter-drone work, with the practical challenge being delivery and deployment.

US creates a federal task force for AI policy

President Trump named national intelligence director Jay Clayton to lead a new federal AI task force, described by the administration as the Super Intelligence Force. The group is meant to coordinate agencies and engage companies, infrastructure providers, public-interest groups and consumers.

The announcement follows a voluntary industry accord and shifts AI oversight closer to the national-security apparatus. The material question is whether the task force develops enforceable policy or primarily coordinates voluntary commitments. Its membership and mandate will shape how the US balances competitiveness, safety, civil concerns and critical-infrastructure exposure.

Major oil exporters keep November production steady

Seven major oil-exporting countries agreed to leave output unchanged in November as the Iran war kept Brent crude above $100 a barrel. The OPEC+ subgroup will meet again on November 1 to assess oil-market conditions.

Holding production steady means producers are not immediately adding supply to counter the disruption that has already lifted fuel costs. The decision now sits beside the G7’s planned release of reserves, making the next few weeks a test of whether stockpile releases can ease pressure without a production response.
