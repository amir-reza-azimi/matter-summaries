Germany and Singapore test digital settlement

The Bundesbank and Singapore's central bank have connected separate experimental settlement systems to exchange euros and Singapore dollars, with DBS, Deutsche Bank and Partior participating in the trial. The payment-versus-payment design links the two currency transfers so that settlement conditions can be checked across systems, using cryptographic proofs without directly integrating the central banks' ledgers.

The project also explored intraday liquidity and securities funding across time zones, making this a test of wholesale financial infrastructure rather than a retail digital currency launch. Its significance is the possibility of connecting separate national systems while preserving their individual design. Commercial deployment remains a further step: legal, regulatory, operational and governance questions still need work.

European factories recover unevenly in September

Eurostat's new September figures show a modest monthly rebound after August's decline, with euro-area industrial production rising by two tenths of a percent and EU output by half a percent. Germany recorded a stronger monthly increase than the euro-area average, while France declined, illustrating how differently national manufacturing sectors are performing.

The annual comparison is less encouraging: output remained below September last year across both the euro area and the EU. Intermediate goods and durable consumer goods improved month to month, but energy production fell and capital goods were flat. The distinction matters because a small monthly recovery does not establish a sustained industrial upswing; the sector and country breakdowns provide a more cautious picture than the headline rebound alone.

EU clarifies carbon border deductions

The European Commission has adopted rules for recognizing carbon prices already paid outside the EU under its carbon border adjustment mechanism. Importers can reduce the certificates they must surrender when an eligible foreign carbon charge has genuinely been paid, but they must document that payment and connect it to the emissions embedded in the imported goods.

Independent certification is central to the new process, with existing CBAM verifiers allowed to certify foreign carbon prices for 2026 and 2027 before a dedicated accreditation regime begins in 2028. For affected importers and overseas producers, the practical development is clearer evidence requirements for avoiding duplicate carbon charges. The first CBAM declarations are due in September 2027; separate default-price values will be published later.

EU imposes new steel import duties

The Commission has imposed anti-dumping duties on certain cold-rolled flat steel imports from India, Japan, Taiwan, Turkey and Vietnam. The rates differ by origin and producer, reaching roughly 28 percent for Japan and some Taiwanese imports, with lower rates for several other suppliers. The measures concern an EU market the Commission values at more than 23 billion euros.

The investigation also used findings of distorted domestic market conditions in India and Taiwan when establishing comparison prices, rather than relying solely on those markets' reported prices. The immediate commercial consequence is a change in the landed cost of covered imports and stronger protection for European producers. The decision therefore matters to steel suppliers and manufacturers sourcing this material, rather than representing a duty on every steel product.

Carbon market coalition sets cooperation agenda

A coalition focused on regulated carbon markets has adopted an Antalya declaration at COP31 and launched working groups to organize cooperation during 2027. Brazil chairs the initiative, with China and the EU as co-chairs; the Commission reports 13 members and 34 observers. Its focus is practical cooperation between existing compliance markets, including their compatibility and possible interoperability.

This does not create a single worldwide carbon price or immediately connect national trading systems. The investment relevance is the direction of regulatory cooperation in markets where companies must pay for emissions, rather than voluntary offset purchasing alone. Separately, the EU is preparing a 2027 proposal on high-quality international credits for its 2040 climate target, which remains a future policy step.

Thousands of customers reported online banking problems on Monday after a software bug in an on-premise JPMorgan Chase system disrupted services. United delayed departures on Monday during an internal system outage that lasted about 100 minutes, while airborne flights and its United Express regional network remained unaffected. The airline reported no indication of a cybersecurity incident and said the problem was resolved. This was a separate airline incident following Friday's nationwide FAA disruption, rather than confirmation that the earlier failure had returned.

business · notable

EU and Kenya advance data agreement

The EU and Kenya have concluded negotiations on mutual data-protection adequacy arrangements and will now begin their respective adoption procedures. If finalized, the arrangement would be the first mutual adequacy framework between the EU and an African country, creating a basis for personal data to move between the two jurisdictions with recognized safeguards.

The distinction between completed negotiations and an adopted decision matters: the announcement does not make the new framework immediately operational. Its potential beneficiaries include businesses providing digital services across borders and organizations collaborating on research, where data-transfer requirements affect everyday operations. The next milestone is formal approval on both sides, building on their wider trade relationship rather than replacing the underlying obligations to protect personal information.

technology · notable

EU publishes product passport registry guidance

New Commission guidance explains how businesses and service providers can connect to the EU's Digital Product Passport Registry, with technical documentation and a testing environment now available. The registry serves as a common index of product identifiers and registration information; the detailed product records remain in decentralized systems controlled by the companies responsible for them.

That architecture matters because a product passport is more than a document uploaded to a central Brussels database. Companies preparing implementation need their own data systems and service providers to exchange consistent information with the shared registry. The new material covers authentication, registration interfaces and data validation, giving implementation teams a more concrete basis for preparation while the testing environment's capabilities continue to develop.

Black Sea talks link security and trade

Ministers from the EU and partners across the Black Sea, South Caucasus and Central Asia met in Brussels to advance cooperation on regional security and transport connections. Discussions linked maritime infrastructure protection, early warning and demining with the development of routes connecting Central Asia to Europe through the Caspian and Black Sea regions.

The economic issue is whether trade corridors can become both more usable and more resilient while war continues to threaten shipping and infrastructure. The Commission highlighted the Black Sea security hub and the Middle Corridor, alongside energy and digital connections, rather than announcing that an entirely new route was already operational. Follow-through now shifts to implementation and a regional connectivity summit planned for Sofia in 2027.
