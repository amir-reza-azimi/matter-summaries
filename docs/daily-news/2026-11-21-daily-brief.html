Berlin asks men to act against violence

Berlin will launch a citywide campaign on Monday encouraging men to intervene against violence against women, including behaviour that precedes physical assaults. Its multilingual guidance addresses situations ranging from sexist comments in group chats to witnessing an attack, and asks men to become active allies rather than silent bystanders. The announcement comes ahead of the international day against violence against women on November 25.

The campaign will run across 400 posters and more than 500 screens, including gyms and shopping centres, alongside 30,000 flyers and postcards. Its practical emphasis is on recognising everyday warning signs and knowing how to respond, extending prevention beyond police intervention after violence has already occurred.

False collection flyers prompt BSR warning

Berlin's sanitation service says flyers promising free bulky waste collection are false and should not be treated as permission to leave furniture or rubbish on the pavement. The notices appeared in Kreuzberg and asked residents to put items outside on Friday morning; BSR cannot rule out their circulation in other neighbourhoods. Its warning also covers electronic waste and other discarded items.

The legitimate alternatives are organised neighbourhood collection days with district offices, free recycling-centre drop-offs for smaller quantities, and a paid collection service for larger loads. The distinction matters because following an unofficial flyer can still result in illegal dumping and substantial fines, rather than a collection by the city.

German factory prices rise 8.1% annually

Germany's industrial producer prices rose eight point one percent in October from a year earlier, the fastest annual increase since February 2023, and increased one point four percent from September. Energy was the main driver, with prices almost nineteen percent above the previous year, while intermediate goods also became substantially more expensive. Excluding energy, the annual increase was three point six percent.

The picture was uneven: machinery and durable consumer goods became dearer, while non-durable consumer goods were cheaper overall and food prices fell. These are prices charged by domestic producers, rather than the consumer inflation rate; the release shows where business cost pressures are building without implying that every increase will pass directly into household prices.

Electricity trade narrows in volume, not cost

Germany exported almost forty-nine terawatt hours of electricity during the first nine months of 2026, a volume increase of more than a quarter compared with the same period last year. Export receipts reached four point three billion euros, while import volumes fell by roughly thirteen percent to just over forty-nine terawatt hours. Austria was the largest destination for exports and Denmark the largest supplier.

Despite buying less electricity, Germany's import bill rose to five point seven billion euros because prices increased. Imports therefore cost more than exports earned even though their physical volumes were close, highlighting why a nearly balanced electricity flow does not mean a balanced trade bill. Import prices per megawatt hour rose faster than export prices.

Russian weapons land in Poland

Two Russian missiles and a suspected drone came down in Poland during Russia's overnight attack on Ukraine, with no immediate reports of injuries or damage. Prime Minister Donald Tusk said the incident appeared to be a deliberate provocation as European forces exercised nearby for a possible postwar Ukraine mission. Moscow had not immediately commented, so intent remained an assessment by Polish officials.

The incidents brought the war onto NATO territory while American-led peace efforts continued, increasing the stakes of airspace protection and European security coordination. EU foreign policy chief Kaja Kallas separately condemned the Polish airspace violation and pointed to civilian deaths and damage to embassies in Kyiv as evidence against Moscow's professed interest in peace.

EU approves €1 billion for Ukraine equipment

The Council approved two decisions releasing another €1 billion through the European Peace Facility for joint purchases of military equipment for Ukraine. The package covers drones, air defence equipment, ammunition, electronic warfare equipment, radar systems and explosives, with deliveries planned by the end of 2027. It is an equipment procurement measure rather than unrestricted financial support to Ukraine's government.

The decisions implement part of the €6.6 billion political agreement reached by member states in September, making this a concrete procurement stage of an existing commitment. The distinction between approval and delivery remains important: the funds now have an authorised route into equipment purchases, but the announcement does not mean all the equipment has arrived on the battlefield.

Europe advances joint disaster and health planning

EU governments agreed a partial negotiating position on combining civil protection and health emergency preparedness under the next long-term budget. Their proposed framework includes medical stockpiles, covering medicines, vaccines, gloves and masks, plus closer civilian and military coordination for disaster response. It would support countries dealing with floods, wildfires, earthquakes and other severe emergencies.

A new analytical and support cell within the existing emergency response coordination centre would track crises affecting several sectors at once. This is a negotiating mandate for talks with the European Parliament, rather than a finished law or an agreed spending package: financial allocations remain part of the separate 2028–2034 budget negotiations. The practical aim is to coordinate supplies, information and operational support across overlapping emergencies.

Incoming House Judiciary chairman Jamie Raskin asked law enforcement agencies to preserve official communications on private messaging apps and investigate possible deletions. economy · notable

Consumer credit protections expand across the EU

The European Commission says expanded consumer credit protections now cover loans up to €100,000 and buy-now-pay-later agreements, bringing more everyday borrowing within the rules. Lenders must give standardised information on rates and total costs, assess whether borrowers can repay sustainably, and allow human review when automated processing is used to evaluate creditworthiness. Certain categories, including mortgages, remain excluded.

Borrowers retain a fourteen-day cancellation period without having to give a reason, while countries must provide debt advice and measures against excessive borrowing costs. The changes address both the sales process and what happens when repayment becomes difficult: clearer advertisements and disclosures should improve comparison, while lenders must exercise forbearance before enforcement. This is a consumer protection update, rather than a recommendation to borrow.

EU gas stocks stand near seventy percent

EU energy officials and industry representatives reported gas storage at around seventy percent and said the system remains prepared for winter despite difficult prices and geopolitical uncertainty. Stored gas has already helped cover demand during the season's first cold spell with low wind, including fuel needed for electricity generation. Officials said greater capacity to import liquefied natural gas provides another buffer against disruptions.

The assessment also considered the planned full phase-out of Russian LNG imports from January 2027, with no immediate supply consequences expected. That is a supply-security judgement, not a promise of low bills: officials still described conditions as challenging and stressed the need to avoid excessive storage depletion before summer while maintaining robust LNG imports.

economy · notable

EU and UK finish carbon market link talks

The EU and UK concluded negotiations to link their emissions trading systems, aiming to apply the agreement from 2028 after ratification and entry into force. Linking the systems could allow goods traded between them to escape their respective carbon border charges, because producers would face connected carbon pricing arrangements. The Commission described this as a possible future exemption, rather than a change already available to exporters.

Several legal steps remain, including amending the EU carbon border regulation's list of exempt countries. For businesses trading covered goods, the significance is reduced risk of double carbon charges and less border administration if those steps are completed. Until then, the announcement does not remove current obligations or guarantee the implementation date.

world · notable

Buildings compact targets a quarter less energy

The EU endorsed a COP31 compact seeking to cut buildings' energy use per square metre by at least twenty-five percent by 2035, measured against 2020. Twenty-four countries backed the initiative, which also supports faster electrification of buildings. Buildings account for roughly a third of global energy consumption, making efficiency relevant to household bills, business costs and dependence on imported fuels.

The target is collective and non-binding on individual countries, so endorsement does not itself require a particular renovation or heating replacement. Progress will be tracked through the existing annual global buildings report, while EU countries continue preparing renovation plans under their own buildings legislation. The next test is whether the shared ambition produces measurable reductions rather than another stand-alone pledge.

France and Germany nominate ECB board candidates

Eurogroup president Kyriakos Pierrakakis received two nominations for upcoming European Central Bank executive board vacancies: Laurence Boone from France and Markus Brunnermeier from Germany. The process follows the notified resignation and end of term of Isabel Schnabel and Philip Lane, respectively. Friday was the deadline for governments to submit candidates, so this announcement identifies the field rather than announcing appointments.

The Council will take the process forward before the European Council makes its decision, with the ECB and European Parliament consulted first. Board membership matters to the institution's leadership, but a nomination alone says nothing definitive about future interest rate decisions. The next concrete development to watch is the formal recommendation and appointment process, rather than assuming either candidate already holds office.

GTA VI posts record launch sales

Rockstar Games estimated that Grand Theft Auto VI generated nearly $3.8 billion in worldwide retail sales on its first day, alongside thirty-two point five million pre-release sales. The figures show demand for the series' first new instalment in thirteen years, far exceeding its predecessor's billion-dollar opening three days.

These are company estimates of retail spending, rather than audited profit. An earlier external estimate was lower, illustrating uncertainty around immediate launch measurements. The commercial test now moves beyond opening demand to sustained sales, as the game becomes a major release for consoles and publishers.
