Iran keeps US talks stalled as both sides prepare for a longer conflict

Iranian officials told Reuters that Tehran is deliberately avoiding a quick agreement with Washington, calculating that a drawn-out confrontation could weaken Donald Trump before the US midterm elections. The two sides remain divided over the order of any steps: Washington wants Iran's nuclear programme addressed early, while Tehran wants sanctions and the maritime blockade eased first. The conflict has kept traffic through the Strait of Hormuz to a trickle and cut Iranian oil exports, while higher energy costs have become a political problem in the United States. With neither side conceding on the core sequencing dispute, negotiators are preserving contact without a near-term breakthrough.

South Korea pulls more Kyiv embassy staff over North Korean POW dispute

South Korea will withdraw additional staff from its embassy in Kyiv, escalating a dispute with Ukraine over two North Korean prisoners of war transferred to Seoul. Seoul says Kyiv broke a confidentiality understanding when Zelenskyy disclosed the transfer at the United Nations, while Ukraine denies that such an agreement existed. South Korea had already recalled its ambassador and says the latest decision also reflects concern over intensified Russian attacks, though essential embassy staff will remain. The clash complicates relations between partners confronting North Korea's military support for Russia, and it shows how the handling of captured soldiers can create a separate diplomatic fault line inside the wider war.

Nigeria confirms an Ebola case linked to East Africa outbreak

Nigeria confirmed that a woman who travelled from Uganda to Abuja has Ebola, making it the first West African country to report a case linked to the current East African outbreak. The patient is isolated and authorities have identified 44 close contacts as they begin tracing and surveillance. The wider outbreak, centred in the Democratic Republic of Congo, has caused thousands of cases and deaths, with infections also reported in Uganda and Kenya. Nigeria successfully contained an earlier outbreak in 2014, but the cross-border movement in this case tests whether that response capacity can again prevent local transmission.

Indonesian wildfires leave Borneo living under a toxic haze

Months of fires in Indonesia have blanketed parts of Borneo in thick smoke, with the haze spreading beyond the country to Singapore, Malaysia and the Philippines. Satellite estimates cited by the BBC put the burned area at about 1.9 million hectares this year, while Indonesia's health ministry has recorded more than 277,000 cases of coughs and acute respiratory infections. Peat and forest fires are releasing particulates, carbon monoxide and greenhouse gases, and families report keeping children indoors or moving classes online. The scale turns a local fire emergency into a regional public-health and climate problem, especially for people who cannot avoid working outdoors.

US importer challenges Trump's Canada tariffs under a rarely used 1930 law

A New Jersey food importer has sued to challenge Donald Trump's use of a Depression-era statute to impose 50% tariffs on 20 billion dollars of Canadian goods. The case tests Section 338 of the 1930 Tariff Act, a power that had never previously been used in a trade dispute, after Canada responded with matching retaliation. The importer argues that later trade laws displaced the old authority and imposed procedural limits on presidential tariff powers. The lawsuit is consequential beyond a single company because it could determine whether the administration has found a durable legal route to broad tariffs after the Supreme Court struck down an earlier tariff programme.

United Airlines forecast fourth-quarter profit well below expectations even as it expects record revenue, because jet fuel costs are rising faster than fares. The carrier expects to pay 4.80 dollars a gallon for fuel, about 45 cents above the market assumption, which it says equates to roughly one dollar a share in earnings. Its outlook reflects a wider squeeze: Delta has cut its earnings forecast, Alaska also expects a larger loss, and airlines are weighing higher prices against limiting capacity. The story matters for travellers and investors because it shows how an energy shock can travel through transport markets, raising fares while narrowing margins.

Data-centre construction is lifting US warehouse demand and rents

AI data-centre projects are creating demand for warehouses and industrial suppliers near large campuses, especially around Dallas-Fort Worth and Washington, DC, according to real-estate firm JLL. Average asking rents rose 3.2% year on year in the third quarter to 10.55 dollars per square foot, the fastest pace since mid-2024. JLL says construction, telecommunications and utility tenants are clustering near the buildout because servers require specialised power, cooling and logistics infrastructure. The spillover is important because it shows that the data-centre race is reshaping physical property markets as well as technology-company budgets, with effects extending beyond the chipmakers and cloud providers financing the facilities.
