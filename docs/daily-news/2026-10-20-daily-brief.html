Trump's approval reaches a new low before US midterms

Donald Trump's approval rating fell to 31%, the lowest of either of his presidential terms, according to a Reuters Ipsos poll completed ahead of the November 3 congressional elections. The poll found that 68% of respondents disapproved of his handling of the economy, including 37% of self-identified Republicans, while Democrats led Republicans on the generic congressional ballot. Polls are snapshots rather than election results, but the combination of economic dissatisfaction, high gasoline prices and a widening party preference creates a harder political environment for Republicans defending their position in Congress.

Houthi strike kills dozens at a Yemen training camp

A Houthi missile attack on the al-Alam security-forces training camp in Yemen's Shabwa province killed more than 50 recruits and injured about 200, according to local authorities reported by Reuters and the BBC. The Iran-aligned Houthis said they targeted Saudi enemy concentrations, while the Saudi-backed government called the strike criminal and promised a response. The attack comes as Yemen's civil war has intensified around the Bab al-Mandab Strait and other fronts. Its scale raises the risk that recent battlefield gains and regional backing will produce a broader cycle of retaliation around a vital shipping chokepoint.

Alberta held a nonbinding referendum on whether to begin the process of separating from Canada, despite polls suggesting the separatist side would lose. Reuters reported that economic grievances and disputes over federal constraints on the province's oil industry have kept the movement alive, even among voters who do not expect immediate nationhood. The vote matters beyond a single provincial result because it adds pressure on Prime Minister Mark Carney as he confronts US tariffs and arguments over Canadian sovereignty. A defeat for separation may not settle the underlying conflict over energy, fiscal policy and Ottawa's authority.

economy · notable

China's growth accelerates as exports offset weak domestic demand

China said its economy grew 4.5% year on year in the July to September quarter, accelerating from the prior quarter and moving closer to its annual target. AP reported that robust exports supported the improvement, while domestic demand remained weak and the International Monetary Fund forecast growth at the lower end of Beijing's target range. The data show why China's external trade position remains central to the global outlook: stronger shipments can support headline growth even when consumer spending and property-related activity are subdued. That imbalance also keeps trade tensions and concerns about export-led excess capacity in focus.

Japan launches a mission to bring back material from Mars' moon

Japan launched a spacecraft toward Mars' small moons on a mission intended to collect material from Phobos and return it to Earth. AP reported that the H3 rocket successfully released the spacecraft, which will study Phobos and Deimos before attempting the sample return. Scientists hope the material can help answer whether the moons are captured asteroids or debris from an ancient impact involving Mars. The mission matters because a successful return would add a new type of sample to planetary science, while demonstrating Japan's growing capability in complex deep-space exploration and autonomous collection.

Cybersecurity report shows how AI agents can scale intrusion attempts

A cybersecurity researcher tracked a Chinese-speaking hacker who used AI agents to attempt around 1,200 break-ins and compromise more than 100 company websites, Reuters reported. The targets included Deere, SharkNinja and Cummins, and the researcher said the attacker outsourced much of the probing and exploitation work to autonomous tools. The story is a practical security warning rather than a claim that AI created a new category of crime. What changes is the cost and scale of execution: comparatively cheap tools can automate the repetitive stages of intrusions, increasing the need for patching, monitoring and incident-response capacity across ordinary corporate websites.

Reuters reported that Google argued the settled-expectations rule had helped drive more patent litigation, after a lower court refused to require reconsideration of its challenge to a software patent.
