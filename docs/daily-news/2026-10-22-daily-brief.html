Critical PHP flaws force governments and major sites offline for emergency patching

Governments and website operators around the world took systems offline after critical flaws in the PHP programming language were disclosed and patches released. PHP underpins much of the public web, including WordPress, Drupal, Joomla and Magento, and cyber agencies warned organisations to update immediately or temporarily shut down exposed services. Reuters reported that some attacks had already been detected, while the BBC said governments, Wikipedia systems and BBC programme sites were among the services affected by the precautionary response. The episode matters because the vulnerabilities had remained hidden for years and were discovered with AI-assisted research, making the race to patch and exploit them unusually fast and broad.

Russia agrees in principle to restart three-way Ukraine talks next month

Russia agreed in principle to resume negotiations with the United States and Ukraine after November 10, following US envoy Steve Witkoff's meeting with Vladimir Putin in St Petersburg. Kremlin adviser Yuri Ushakov said the talks would focus on a postwar security system for Russia and Ukraine, while Volodymyr Zelenskyy said Kyiv supported a rapid restart. Earlier rounds this year produced little beyond prisoner exchanges, and the announcement came as missile alerts again sounded over Kyiv. This is a material update to the Oslo diplomacy: it moves the discussion from a proposed channel to a stated Russian commitment, but leaves the longstanding disagreements over territory, security and the terms of any settlement intact.

US orders some diplomats out of Saudi Arabia as Houthi attacks intensify

The United States ordered non-emergency diplomatic staff and family members to leave Saudi Arabia as attacks linked to Yemen's Houthi movement intensified. Saudi authorities said strikes and intercepted-drone debris had killed three people and injured others, while airports, oil facilities and other critical infrastructure have faced repeated missile and drone attacks. The Houthis say their campaign responds to Saudi actions and a blockade, while the violence has also disrupted routes around the Red Sea and added to pressure on global oil prices. The departure order is a concrete sign that Washington sees a risk of rapid escalation, not just a continuing regional security problem.

world · notable

UN report says climate pledges now point to a hotter world than last year

A new United Nations assessment says even if countries meet their current emissions pledges, the planet is likely to warm 2.6 degrees Celsius above pre-industrial levels by 2100, worse than the previous projection. The report attributes much of the deterioration to the United States leaving the Paris Agreement, higher US emissions and renewed coal generation, while most other major economies reduced emissions or slowed their growth. The world is already about 1.4 degrees warmer and the UN says the 1.5-degree goal is no longer achievable without later removing carbon from the atmosphere. The report makes the cost of delay more concrete for policy makers, energy markets and communities exposed to climate risks.

High oil prices spread from petrol pumps into food, heating and mortgages

Oil at about 110 dollars a barrel and gas prices at their highest levels since late 2022 are feeding through to household budgets well beyond transport, Reuters reported. Higher fuel and heating costs add to food, packaging and clothing prices, while energy-driven inflation keeps pressure on borrowing costs and mortgage payments. The report cites record US diesel prices, sharply higher British heating bills and rising Australian loan repayments as central banks respond to inflation. The broader significance is that an energy shock can become a cost-of-living shock even for households that do not directly buy much fuel, making economic policy and consumer demand more sensitive to events in energy-producing regions.

economy · notable

US bank regulators allow crypto platforms to keep offering stablecoin rewards

Federal bank regulators said cryptocurrency exchanges can continue offering interest-like rewards to customers who hold stablecoins, despite bank-industry warnings that the practice could pull deposits from traditional lenders. The new guidance keeps stablecoin issuers themselves from paying interest, but permits exchanges to pass through earnings from the assets backing the coins on a case-by-case basis. Banks argue that higher crypto yields could weaken their ability to fund loans, while consumer advocates warn users may mistake the rewards for insured bank products. The decision is important because it preserves a competitive advantage for crypto platforms as the United States implements its new stablecoin framework.

Meta ordered to pay 25 billion dollars in New Mexico privacy case

A New Mexico judge ordered Meta to pay 25 billion dollars after a jury found the company misled consumers about the privacy of their Facebook data. The case traces back to allegations that Cambridge Analytica obtained user data without consent, and the judge said a much larger penalty was technically warranted under state law before reducing it. Meta said it would appeal and argued the ruling made it difficult to do business in the state. The decision is one of the largest corporate penalties in US history and adds a substantial legal overhang as Meta faces other lawsuits over data practices and the safety of its platforms.
