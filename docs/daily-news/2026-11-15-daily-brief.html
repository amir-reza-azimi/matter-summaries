US raises NATO membership in exchange for a Ukrainian withdrawal from Donbas

American negotiators asked whether Ukraine would withdraw from the part of Donbas it still controls if it could join NATO, President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said. He described this as the first explicit link between alliance membership and territorial concessions in the talks, while maintaining that Kyiv would not simply surrender territory. Any comprehensive package would need security guarantees, support for a strong Ukrainian army and reconstruction funding before he put it to a national referendum.

The proposal remains exploratory, with no agreement announced, and Russia has long opposed Ukrainian NATO membership. Zelenskyy also rejected negotiating a new European security order before ending the war. The immediate issue is whether Washington can turn these incompatible positions into concrete guarantees and a negotiating agenda.

FAA probes a router failure as airlines work through the shutdown backlog

Officials are investigating whether a router installed during L3Harris maintenance triggered the communications instability behind Friday’s nearly five-hour nationwide flight shutdown. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said investigators had not determined whether faulty equipment or installation errors were responsible; L3Harris is assisting. Reuters reported that the disruption had affected more than 20,000 flights since Friday, while the FAA expected schedules to normalize by late Sunday.

The investigation now goes beyond restoring departures. Officials said new routers flooded other equipment with connection attempts, disrupting data across much of the air traffic network. The episode exposes the difficulty of replacing decades-old infrastructure while keeping it running. For passengers, a lifted ground stop still leaves aircraft, crews and bookings out of position.

Hungary recovers nearly $20 million in a former minister’s bribery investigation

Hungarian authorities said they found cash buried in forests and hidden in farm buildings, warehouses and a residential property during a bribery investigation involving former development minister Miklos Sesztak. Finance Minister Andras Karman said a dozen treasury workers needed eight hours to count it. Sesztak denies wrongdoing, and his lawyer told local media the seized money was not his and that other suspects were involved.

The case is part of Prime Minister Peter Magyar’s anti-corruption campaign after defeating Viktor Orban in April. Sesztak’s Christian Democrat parliamentary leader has called on him to surrender his seat while clearing his name. The recovery is a concrete investigative development, but ownership of the money and criminal responsibility remain matters for the legal process.

technology · major

Starship returns from orbit and is caught by its launch tower for the first time

SpaceX recovered Starship’s upper stage with giant mechanical arms at its Texas launch tower after roughly 22 hours in orbit. The spacecraft completed 14 circuits of Earth and deployed 26 Starlink satellites before re-entry. Its successful return is a major advance beyond Friday’s launch, when the Super Heavy booster failed to make its own planned landing, preventing the hoped-for recovery of both stages.

Catching the spacecraft demonstrates a crucial part of SpaceX’s reusable-rocket design, but routine reuse is still unproven. The heat shield still needs improvements to reduce refurbishment, and the system has yet to recover both stages on one mission. For lunar missions, another difficult milestone remains: transferring fuel between spacecraft in orbit.

Italy fines Meta €805 million over access to Android photo galleries

Italy’s data protection authority said Meta accessed Android users’ photos and videos without adequate consent or information through a Facebook feature suggesting gallery content to share. The regulator imposed an €805 million fine and gave the company 60 days to disable the feature by default and obtain separate consent. It said around 20 million Android users in Italy were affected.

Meta disputes the finding, says users must already grant device permission and plans to appeal. The investigation did not cover potential use of the material for training AI models, which Meta denied, and the authority reserved a separate assessment of a newer cloud-processing feature. The dispute centers on whether ordinary device permission provides sufficient authorization for this additional use of personal galleries.
