Spain declares mourning after high-speed train collision kills at least 68

Spain declared mourning after a collision between high-speed trains near Urda killed at least 68 people, according to the Associated Press. The crash happened on a major rail corridor and quickly became a national emergency, with rescue, identification and passenger-support work still under way. Officials said the cause had not been established. The transport minister said investigators were examining the signalling and operating sequence rather than assigning blame to individual engineers. The scale of the death toll has put pressure on authorities to establish a clear account of how a collision could occur on a high-speed network built around tightly controlled traffic management.

Iran pressure intensifies the global gas squeeze

The Financial Times reported that pressure around Iran was tightening the global gas market. The story is distinct from the immediate European price move because it concerns the broader supply and transport system connecting producers, shipping lanes and import-dependent economies. Any prolonged disruption can redirect cargoes, raise competition for flexible liquefied natural gas and expose countries with limited alternatives. The effects are uneven: producers may gain pricing power, while importers face higher procurement costs and a more difficult planning environment. Policymakers will be watching whether the risk remains a market premium or develops into measurable supply losses that require emergency responses.
