Oregon elementary-school shooting kills four

A 16-year-old opened fire at Silver Rail Elementary School in Bend, Oregon, killing three children and a teacher before being killed by an armed community member, police said. A seven-year-old student and a teacher were taken to hospital with critical injuries. Police said the attacker fired through locked doors to enter the campus, while the community member pursued him as officers arrived. The episode again puts school security and American gun policy under scrutiny, but the immediate focus is the victims, the local investigation, and what authorities can establish about the shooter’s access and motive.

UN leadership frontrunner calls for a less risk-averse institution

Rebeca Grynspan, the Costa Rican economist seen as a frontrunner for the next United Nations secretary-general, said the organisation must accept the risk of diplomatic failure rather than retreat from hard mediation. She argued that job cuts alone would weaken a body already facing shrinking resources and declining political influence. Her proposal combines a more proactive peace role with fewer duplicated functions and a sharper mandate. The intervention matters ahead of a Security Council preference poll because it frames the succession as a choice about whether the UN can reform while major-power conflicts have repeatedly sidelined it.

Pakistan school roof collapse kills 34 children and a teacher

A roof collapse at a privately run school in Peshawar killed at least 34 children and a teacher during morning classes, with dozens more pupils injured. Rescue teams pulled children from the rubble for hours, while police said an overloaded upper structure had failed onto classrooms below. The school owner was arrested and authorities opened an investigation after relatives said warnings about the building had been ignored. Beyond the immediate tragedy, the case exposes safety and oversight failures in low-income education settings, where families have little ability to absorb the consequences of weak construction standards.

Houthi missiles strike Yemen’s presidential-palace complex

Yemen’s Houthi movement fired missiles at the Maashiq presidential-palace complex in Aden, killing two security guards, according to government officials. The prime minister and cabinet members who were at the compound were unharmed. Houthi officials said the intended target was a Saudi military operations headquarters and weapons depots, a claim Reuters could not independently verify. The attack brings the conflict directly to the seat of Yemen’s internationally recognised government and underlines how a wider regional confrontation can turn political sites, civilian life, and military infrastructure into immediate targets with limited warning.

Ukraine resumes refinery strikes after abandoning energy truce

Ukraine struck a Rosneft refinery southeast of Moscow after President Volodymyr Zelenskiy said Kyiv was no longer bound by the energy-site pause announced by Donald Trump. The Ryazan facility accounts for nearly five percent of Russian refining throughput, making the strike significant for domestic fuel supply as well as military signalling. Kyiv said Russian missile and drone attacks on its power system had continued during the supposed pause. This is a material update to the earlier ceasefire story: the restraint has ended, and renewed attacks on refining infrastructure risk widening both the battlefield and global fuel-price effects.

KFC switches US soda supply from PepsiCo to Coca-Cola

KFC will replace PepsiCo with Coca-Cola as beverage supplier at its 3,718 United States restaurants, ending a relationship that reaches back roughly four decades. Coca-Cola products are due to arrive by spring 2027, while PepsiCo said it had renewed agreements for Taco Bell and KFC International. Financial terms were not disclosed, but the decision removes a large, visible fountain-drink account as PepsiCo works to revive North American soda performance. The change is a useful read on bargaining power in food service, where distribution agreements connect consumer brands, restaurant traffic, and long-running corporate relationships.

Global electric-vehicle sales fall as Europe diverges from China and North America

Global electric-vehicle sales fell two percent in September, ending six consecutive months of growth, according to Benchmark Mineral Intelligence data. Europe posted its strongest month on record, with almost 600,000 vehicles sold, but that was outweighed by an eighteen percent drop in China and a forty-four percent decline in North America. Researchers linked North America’s fall to comparisons with buying before federal tax credits expired, while Chinese producers increasingly rely on exports. The split matters for carmakers, battery suppliers, and European policymakers because the market is no longer moving as one global demand story.

Google warns of broad exploitation risk in BeyondTrust flaw

Google’s threat-intelligence team warned organisations to patch or mitigate a newly disclosed vulnerability in BeyondTrust Remote Support, an enterprise tool used to access and troubleshoot devices remotely. Google said a suspected China-linked group known as Armory Castle had exploited the flaw in August before public disclosure. Successful exploitation can expose credentials, permit arbitrary file uploads, and give attackers control of a host, according to the warning. This deserves attention beyond a routine software update because remote-support tools sit near privileged systems, and public disclosure can prompt copycat activity by other attackers.

US wholesale inflation accelerates on fuel and transport costs

United States producer prices rose six point three percent year on year in September, the largest increase since late 2022, as fuel and transport costs climbed sharply. The monthly increase was one point three percent, with diesel, freight, warehousing, and some food costs all adding pressure before prices reach consumers. The data reinforced a hot consumer-inflation reading and raises the stakes for the Federal Reserve’s upcoming decision. The important question is whether energy and logistics costs stay contained or keep spreading through supply chains into core goods, household budgets, and rate expectations.

England and Wales set new criminal limits on violent pornography

England and Wales will ban the possession or publication of pornography depicting strangulation, incestuous activity, or adults role-playing as children under 16 from early November. The government said the rules follow a review that found depictions of choking widespread on major pornography sites and linked them to harmful normalisation. Publication offences can carry up to five years in prison, while certain possession offences can also lead to custody. The policy will test how quickly platforms and regulators can remove prohibited material, and it adds a concrete legal boundary to the broader debate about online safety and sexual violence.
