Macron calls for calm after unrest across France

President Emmanuel Macron appealed for calm after protests over prices and public services escalated into some of France's worst unrest in years. Reuters reported fresh clashes in Paris and Marseille, more than 2,000 arrests, looted or damaged businesses, and a town hall largely destroyed by arson. Macron acknowledged that peaceful protesters had legitimate grievances while saying violence had no justification. The update turns the previous day's demonstrations into a broader political test: it exposes social anger, weak economic confidence and the pressure facing Macron before the 2027 election, when opposition parties will seek to turn that anger into votes.

Canadian military base fire kills four soldiers

A rapidly spreading grass fire at Canadian Forces Base Wainwright killed four soldiers and left 19 people in critical condition, Reuters reported. Officials said winds of up to 96 kilometres per hour turned a smouldering fire in a training area into a fast-moving emergency, with flames advancing as much as a kilometre within an hour. Thirty-nine people were in hospital and air ambulances were deployed from Edmonton and Saskatoon. Prime Minister Mark Carney called it one of Canada's worst peacetime military tragedies, placing both the emergency response and the training conditions under immediate scrutiny.

Synthetic drug mixtures deepen US overdose crisis

BBC reporting from Columbus, Ohio describes an expanding market for homemade synthetic drugs, including fentanyl mixed with synthetic tranquillisers that make overdose reversals harder. Fentanyl is already far more potent than heroin, while new combinations can be produced locally and sold cheaply. The report follows users, recovery workers and law-enforcement officers to show how a shift in supply is changing the practical challenge for public health services. The key point is not simply a continuing opioid crisis: increasingly variable mixtures make treatment, prevention and rapid emergency response less predictable for already stretched local services.

China has proposed export controls on 12 technologies used to make solar equipment, saying wider oversight is needed where those technologies have national-security applications. Reuters reported that the proposed changes form part of a broader revision covering areas including AI and biotechnology. Rising mortgage costs begin to weigh on UK housing affordability

UK asking prices rose in October, but Rightmove said higher mortgage rates were starting to reduce affordability, even in previously resilient and lower-priced parts of the market. Reuters reported that the average two-year fixed rate climbed to about five and a half percent in October, from just over five percent a month earlier and about four and a quarter percent in February, while asking prices were slightly lower than a year earlier. The monthly price rise was also below the usual October average. Housing is a key transmission channel for higher borrowing costs, so the data points to pressure on first-time buyers, sellers and consumer confidence before the government's budget.
