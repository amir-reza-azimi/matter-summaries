US flights resume after nationwide air-traffic system failure

American aviation regulators lifted a nationwide ground stop after communications problems forced thousands of flights to remain on the ground. Officials said new technology had overloaded older parts of the air-traffic system, causing intermittent radar and communications gaps; they reported no evidence of a cyberattack. Aircraft already airborne landed safely, but restoring departures does not immediately repair disrupted crew and aircraft schedules. The shutdown exposed the practical risk of modernising an essential network while continuing to operate ageing equipment. Passengers face a gradual recovery, while the administration is pressing Congress to fund a much larger overhaul of aviation infrastructure.

Trump, Xi and Putin plan joint talks in Shenzhen

The White House confirmed plans for Donald Trump, Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin to meet Thursday alongside the APEC summit in Shenzhen. Trump wants to discuss Iran and a possible agreement covering the three countries’ nuclear arsenals, while an American official said Ukraine would not be on his planned agenda. The last Russian-American nuclear treaty expired in February, leaving a significant gap in arms-control limits. Analysts cautioned that this unprecedented three-way gathering may produce more symbolism than substance. The important question is whether a meeting can turn broad diplomatic ambitions into a negotiating process with specific commitments and follow-up.

Europe agrees on a team for Ukraine peace negotiations

European leaders agreed that Britain, France, Germany, Italy and Poland, alongside EU institutions, will represent the continent in Ukraine peace discussions. NATO is also expected to participate, with national security advisers likely to form the core of the team. The decision settles a practical representation question that had complicated Europe’s response to American-led diplomacy. At the same time, Friedrich Merz and other leaders rejected Russia’s attempt to make a wider redesign of European security a prerequisite for addressing the war. The next test is whether Europe can maintain one negotiating position without letting institutional arguments displace efforts to achieve a ceasefire.

Dutch coalition enters crisis talks after wealth-tax split

Prime Minister Rob Jetten said Dutch coalition leaders would continue talks over the weekend to prevent their minority government from collapsing. The immediate dispute concerns a wealth-tax concession used to obtain opposition support for the government’s 2027 tax plan. The pro-business VVD voted against its own government’s package, while Jetten’s D66 and the Christian Democrats backed it; opposition votes secured passage. That exposes a structural weakness of a coalition without a majority in either parliamentary chamber. Leaders must now decide whether they can restore workable cooperation, or whether the disagreement over tax policy has made the government’s broader programme impossible.

Supreme Court takes up Trump’s southern-border asylum restrictions

The American Supreme Court agreed to hear the administration’s challenge to decisions finding its southern-border asylum restrictions unlawful. Trump’s order described conditions at the border as an invasion and sought to block applications categorically. Federal law permits people who fear persecution to seek asylum regardless of how they entered the country, while the administration argues its authority to deny asylum also permits it to refuse applications it expects to reject. The justices have accepted the dispute, rather than decided its merits. Their eventual ruling could define the relationship between statutory access to asylum and presidential power to restrict entry.

AI financing becomes the leading concern in the Fed’s stability survey

A Federal Reserve survey found 87% of financial-market contacts identified artificial intelligence as a salient stability risk, up from half in May. Respondents cited cyberattacks, complex financing and the possibility that disappointing AI earnings could trigger a wider correction. The accompanying report examined advanced-computing debt issuance reaching $680 billion by October and exposures through debt, guarantees and future leases potentially exceeding $3 trillion. These findings describe vulnerabilities rather than predict an imminent crash. The concern is that interconnected financing could spread losses or conceal concentrations of exposure if the infrastructure boom slows, especially while asset valuations and hedge-fund leverage remain high.

business · notable

SEC proposes a substantial reduction in executive-pay disclosure

The American securities regulator proposed requiring most covered companies to disclose detailed compensation packages for only their chief executive and chief financial officer, rather than five executives. It would also raise the threshold for reporting perks from $10,000 to $150,000 and reduce several disclosure tables. The SEC says the changes would remove complexity that adds little useful information, while investor advocates argue shareholders would lose visibility into management rewards. A 60-day public comment period comes before any final decision. The proposal therefore creates a concrete corporate-governance debate over which pay details investors need to judge incentives, performance and accountability.

American bank overdraft fees reach a four-year quarterly high

American banks collected $1.6 billion in overdraft fees during the third quarter, about 9% more than in the preceding quarter, according to Reuters calculations confirmed by a banking-data provider. Economists said rising overdrafts could offer an early warning of pressure on household finances, although other credit indicators have not deteriorated in the same way. The increase also follows the reversal of a planned federal fee cap and changes in consumer-finance oversight. Several banks say grace periods prevent charges on most overdrawing transactions. The figures warrant attention as both a consumer-cost issue and a possible sign of stress, rather than proof of a broad credit downturn.

Starship reaches orbit, but booster loss prevents a double catch

SpaceX’s Starship reached orbit and deployed 26 Starlink satellites, while the returning booster was diverted offshore and exploded instead of being caught at the launch site. The spacecraft was expected to attempt its own return on Saturday after a flight of up to 22 hours. That leaves the test with mixed results: useful orbital operations succeeded, but the planned recovery of both rocket stages became impossible. Full reuse is central to SpaceX’s ambition for frequent launches supporting lunar and Mars missions. The next milestone is the spacecraft’s return, followed by understanding why a previously demonstrated booster-catching procedure failed this time.

EU backs Poland’s stronger energy plan while identifying implementation gaps

The European Commission said Poland’s updated national energy and climate plan could deliver its 2030 targets if fully implemented. Its renewable-energy and efficiency contributions now align with the wider EU ambition, but the assessment calls for additional action in heating, cooling, transport and industry. It also identifies a projected shortfall in land-use carbon removals, urges stronger building-efficiency policies and recommends phasing out fossil-fuel subsidies. Poland was the last member state to submit its updated plan. The distinction is between improved targets on paper and policies capable of delivering them, with buildings and sector-specific renewable deployment now central to the implementation challenge.

EU outlines Mediterranean climate investment and resilience initiatives

At COP31, the European Commission presented climate actions under its Pact for the Mediterranean, linking regional cooperation with investment in resilience and a cleaner economy. The programme addresses interconnected pressures from heat, drought, water scarcity and biodiversity loss that affect food, energy and infrastructure. Initiatives include a clean-transition partnership, investment through the Blue Mediterranean framework and support for aquatic producers and small-scale fisheries. These are proposed channels for cooperation and investment, rather than evidence that projects have already delivered results. The practical test is whether regional commitments translate into finance and locally implemented adaptation that reduces exposure to shared climate risks.
