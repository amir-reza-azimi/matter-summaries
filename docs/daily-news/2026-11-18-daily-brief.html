Berlin prepares winter road services

Berlin's cleaning authority says about 2,400 staff and 540 snow-clearing and spreading vehicles are ready for icy conditions, with stocks of salt, brine and grit replenished. Crews normally work two shifts, adding a third during extreme weather, while urban highways, major roads and public-transport routes receive particular priority.

The service also covers cycle paths, crossings and roughly 6,300 bus and tram stops, but municipal readiness does not mean every pavement is handled by the same crews. The city's update distinguishes those routes from sidewalks generally cleared by the responsible residents, with exceptions such as paths beside public parks. Taken together, the figures show how Berlin intends to prioritize road safety and keep buses and trams moving when snow and ice arrive.

Berlin renews stove pollution campaign

Berlin has reopened registration for its free online stove-use courses as part of the city's Clean Air Plan. The roughly hour-long sessions cover dry firewood, storage, lighting and airflow, because fuel choice and everyday operation influence how much wood is consumed and how much particulate pollution reaches surrounding neighborhoods.

The scale is larger than an occasional fireplace: the Senate estimates about 115,000 wood-burning stoves across Berlin. Since the campaign began in 2023, around 5,400 people have registered and about 2,600 have completed it; the program is due to continue through the 2028–2029 heating season. The renewed offer is an air-quality intervention through operating practices, with voluntary participation and course certificates recording completion; uptake so far covers a small proportion of stove owners.

germany · notable

Germany expands industrial power relief

The Commission has approved changes allowing Germany to expand compensation for electricity-related emissions costs and, temporarily, combine it with separate power-price relief. The emissions-cost scheme will cover additional eligible industrial sectors and offer simpler access for smaller firms, with an estimated average budget increase of 1.6 billion euros annually for costs incurred between 2026 and 2030.

The separate electricity-relief program gains 1.7 billion euros across its 2026–2028 duration. A temporary exception permits aid under both programs for part of eligible consumption during March–December 2026, relaxing the normal prohibition on overlapping support. This matters to energy-intensive manufacturers competing against cheaper-power jurisdictions: the change expands permitted assistance, subject to defined eligibility rules and limits on the companies and consumption covered by each program.

economy · notable

Europe forecasts growth with persistent inflation

The Commission's autumn outlook projects continued expansion despite expensive energy and tighter financing, with EU growth of 1.4 percent in both 2026 and 2027; the euro area is expected to grow more slowly, at 1.2 percent in each year. Resilient consumption and recovering goods exports have supported activity, while investment has been less strong than expected.

The harder part of the outlook is purchasing power: euro-area inflation is forecast to remain above three percent on an annual basis through 2027, with a near-term peak above four percent before easing. Food-price pressures are expected to intensify even as energy inflation moderates. These are projections, but their combination points to a business environment with modest demand growth and continued cost pressure rather than rapid relief for households.

economy · notable

US retail spending slows

October retail sales increased 0.2 percent, slowing from September's 0.8 percent gain as shoppers pulled back on cars, clothing and furniture. Gas-station spending rose, while sales excluding that category barely increased, showing how higher prices can support dollar totals without indicating stronger purchasing activity. The report arrives before the holiday shopping season, when retailers are relying on discounts to attract cautious households. It also covers goods more comprehensively than services, so it is a partial view of consumption. The useful distinction is between money spent and the volume of purchases, especially when inflation strains budgets.

economy · notable

EU agrees faster energy permitting

Council and Parliament negotiators reached an overnight provisional agreement to simplify approvals for renewable generation, electricity grids and storage. The proposal calls for clearer deadlines and digital application portals, while allowing member states to treat some unanswered intermediate administrative steps as approved. It also sets principles for dialogue and sharing benefits with communities hosting larger renewable projects.

The target is a structural bottleneck: the Council says transmission projects can take around a decade, with permitting consuming more than half that time. Faster approvals could help generation and network capacity develop together, but the agreement still requires endorsement and formal adoption. Governments would then have eighteen months to transpose the directive, putting formal adoption and national implementation between this agreement and its full effects on planning applications.

investment · notable

Dutch wind projects gain funding framework

The Commission has approved a Dutch support scheme with a maximum budget of about 9.45 billion euros for two North Sea wind farms with a combined capacity of two gigawatts. Tenders are planned before July 2027, with operations expected in 2032 and support potentially lasting fifteen years after generation starts.

The design matters as much as the headline sum: subsidy-free bids offering a concession payment get priority, and public support is considered only if that route does not succeed. Any aid would be a variable premium supplementing market revenue, with safeguards against excessive compensation and provisions to recover some payments when prices rise. Approval therefore creates a framework for competitive development and financial risk-sharing; it does not mean the entire budget has been paid or construction is complete.

Nvidia grows while resetting leadership

Nvidia reported quarterly revenue of 115.8 billion dollars, up 103 percent from a year earlier, with data-center sales accounting for 107.6 billion dollars. The results show continued expansion in computing demand through a leadership crisis; AP independently reported the earnings and the board's rejection of Jensen Huang's offer to return, leaving Colette Kress in charge while a permanent chief is sought.

The company also created a board committee overseeing national security and export compliance, with its relevant teams reporting to that committee and Kress. The combined development is substantive: strong commercial results are arriving alongside a formal change in oversight. The next test is whether the company maintains execution and customer confidence as leadership and compliance responsibilities settle, with the transition continuing beyond this quarterly reporting cycle.

technology · notable

Waymo suspends freeway service

Waymo disclosed that it has stopped driverless freeway rides across all its markets after California restricted freeway operations over safety incidents. The suspension began last week, according to the company, so Tuesday's report establishes its scope rather than announcing a same-day shutdown. California cited vehicles entering construction zones and a separate encounter with highway patrol vehicles. Waymo said the construction encounters caused no injuries or collisions and continues freeway testing with trained specialists at the wheel. City-street service is distinct. The operational question is what corrective measures will satisfy regulators before unrestricted freeway rides can return.

An internal NSA network carrying classified communications went offline on Tuesday, according to a defense official, while the agency described temporary degradation during scheduled maintenance. The NSA said mission-critical capabilities remained operational, but provided little detail on the disruption's duration or scope. Officials also said there was no evidence connecting this episode to recent airline, banking and software outages or attributing them to hostile actors. The unresolved issue is operational resilience during upgrades, while the precise technical cause still requires investigation.

Lawmakers advance aircraft locator requirement

US lawmakers agreed on requiring civilian aircraft to carry equipment that helps pilots see nearby traffic, while military provisions remain unresolved. The announcement followed campaigning by families of the 67 people killed in the January 2025 Washington midair collision. Aircraft in busy airspace already broadcast their location, but the complementary equipment displaying nearby aircraft is not universally required. That gap explains the proposed safety change. The agreement is progress toward a compromise bill, with sponsors hoping for passage this year; it does not mean aircraft must immediately install equipment or that final legislation is complete.

world · notable

EU advances Syria reconstruction cooperation

The EU and Syria convened their recovery coordination forum in Damascus for the first time, bringing together governments, development banks and civil society to discuss practical investment conditions. The visit launched an institutional technical-assistance hub and included new EU-funded contracts focused on the rule of law, the business environment and support for smaller companies.

Syria also provided a signed commitment toward implementing the EU's Registered Exporter system, a step that could help Syrian products qualify for preferential market access. These are specific institution-building and trade preparations aimed at improving the conditions needed for larger investments to become viable. Their significance is the effort to turn diplomatic re-engagement into functioning institutions, financeable businesses and exports, with implementation still determining the benefits on the ground.

world · notable

EU sets lead gunshot phaseout

The Commission has published a new restriction on the sale and use of gunshot containing at least one percent lead, following adoption on Monday. The rules will apply from December 2033, providing a long transition for hunters, sport shooters and shooting ranges to adapt. Shooting at appropriately contained ranges is excepted, and military and security uses are outside the restriction.

The environmental issue is the accumulation of toxic lead in soil, wildlife and food chains, including exposure through game meat and water. The Commission cites estimates that the measure could prevent roughly 550,000 tonnes of lead emissions over twenty years. The announcement creates a clear future product boundary for ammunition suppliers and civilian users, while the exemptions and implementation date limit its immediate effect.
