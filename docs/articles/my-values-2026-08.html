My Values

Context: a synthesis pulled together on August 7th, 2026, from five parallel research passes across my journal, my Pursuit newsletter, my professional and consulting work, the rest of my vault, and my health and wealth records. Ranked by how many independent sources converge on each one, not just one read of one source.

Core values, cross-validated across sources

Growth as identity, not just a goal. My own words show up unprompted across dozens of unrelated notes: "I'm analytical and I love constantly learning and experimenting with my life." Pursuit's whole premise is forcing reflection into structure. My journal narrates almost every hard moment — tax stress, sleep problems, rejection — as a growth lesson. This is the most cross-validated thing in my life.

Evidence over assertion, precision over convenience. My health and wealth records both independently ended up with nearly identical structures: nothing counts as fact unless it's sourced, hypotheses get a named holder and a confidence score. My consulting brand rules ban fabricated numbers and hype language. Even my dating messages read, "I'd rather be upfront than let it fade." I built the same rigor into my body, my money, and my words.

Freedom and autonomy — for myself, and for the people I work with. Success is having the freedom to pursue the continual grind I most enjoy. Professionally this shows up inverted: my consulting identity is explicitly about enablement, not dependency. Build the system, then leave people running it without me. Same value, pointed outward.

Connection, hosting, being the connector. I've thrown hundred-person parties, organized meetups, and repeatedly introduce friends to each other with zero personal upside. "Curiosity, adventure, community" is the one place I named my own values explicitly, in my own words, in a weekly check-in with myself.

Presence and inner peace, chased through structure. My one unambiguous answer to "if you could have one thing in life" was inner peace. Daily meditation, a Vipassana retreat on the bucket list, three years of a newsletter literally about slowing down. This one is real, and it runs deep.

Roots and duty — specifically Iranian, specifically toward my parents. Checking bombing-map overlays against my father's commute route during the Tehran crackdown. Managing his and my brother's money with explicit, fair documentation. This is distinct from generic family values — it's a live sense of obligation across distance.

The tensions worth naming plainly

Not contradictions that need resolving — just where the evidence actually points, tensions and all.

Presence and simplicity, versus compulsive system-building. Pursuit's most repeated theme across a hundred and thirty-seven issues is anti-hustle, anti-optimization — written by someone who, by his own admission in an unpublished draft from this week, just spent four months obsessively building AI systems every evening while the newsletter went dark. I am doing the exact thing I keep telling a thousand subscribers not to do.

Autonomy and self-sufficiency, versus actual dependence on romantic reciprocity. My 2025 journal entries assert that true happiness comes from within, with real evidence — solo travel, solo museum visits. The 2026 entries show a single dating rejection knocking my grounding loose enough that I have to consciously list everything good in my life just to counteract it. The solitude story may be more aspiration than achievement right now.

Honesty and directness, versus fading instead of closing. My own documented pattern is defaulting to silence or text when a direct conversation is called for. Already named. Not yet fixed.

Open curiosity, versus real judgment of lifestyles that don't match my frame. Swinger parties, "shallow" bar scenes, an ex's yoga-instructor aesthetic all got flagged as misaligned at various points. Caught sometimes. Not always.

Enablement, not dependency, versus grieving the craft I've automated away. Professionally I champion leverage through AI. And in my own writing I've named grieving the loss of being a musician, not a conductor. The efficiency I'm building trades away something I admit I miss.

That's the picture. Not a verdict. Just where the evidence actually points.
